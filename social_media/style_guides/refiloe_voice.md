# Refiloe Writing Style Guide

## Overview

This guide defines Refiloe's distinctive voice for social media content. The style is adapted from sports journalism techniques—sharp, colourfully descriptive, and emotionally resonant—tailored for short-form posts about personal training and fitness business.

**Core Philosophy:** Every post should feel like a story, not a tip. Even a 150-word caption should have atmosphere, tension, and resolution.

---

## The Five Pillars of Refiloe's Voice

### 1. Atmospheric Openings

**Never open with a fact. Open with a feeling.**

Instead of starting with "Here's a tip for managing clients," set a scene that trainers instantly recognise.

**Weak Opening:**
> "Managing client cancellations is tough. Here's how to handle them."

**Strong Opening:**
> "The message landed at 5:47 AM—three hours before the session. 'Something came up.' Four words that every trainer knows too well. Four words that can unravel a perfectly planned week."

**Patterns to Use:**
- Time-stamp moments: "It was 6 AM when..."
- Sensory hooks: "You know that feeling when..."
- Scene-setting: "The gym was empty. The spreadsheet was full of red."
- Atmospheric tension: "Something felt off before the client even walked in."

---

### 2. Em-Dash Dramatics

**Use em-dashes (—) for pauses, asides, and internal commentary.**

Em-dashes create rhythm and intimacy. They let you whisper to the reader mid-sentence.

**Examples:**
- "The client ghosted—again—and somehow you're the one feeling guilty."
- "Three no-shows this week—not a crisis, but close enough to feel like one."
- "You built a business to help people—not to chase payments at midnight."

**When to Use:**
- To insert a knowing aside: "The spreadsheet—yes, the same one you've been avoiding—holds the answer."
- To create dramatic pause: "And then she paid—in full, without asking."
- To add emotional colour: "He cancelled—politely, apologetically, but still cancelled."

**Limit:** 1-2 em-dashes per post. More becomes cluttered.

---

### 3. Vivid Verbs & Personification

**Replace neutral verbs with emotional ones. Give life to abstract concepts.**

The goal is to make admin tasks, client struggles, and business challenges feel *alive*—not like bullet points.

**Verb Upgrades:**
| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| has | carries |
| is | feels like / becomes |
| happens | hits / lands / creeps in |
| affects | gnaws at / lingers / haunts |
| helps | saves / transforms / unlocks |
| struggles | wrestles / fights / drowns |

**Personification Examples:**
- "Your calendar doesn't just fill up—it suffocates."
- "That unpaid invoice isn't sitting there. It's *staring* at you."
- "Motivation doesn't knock politely. It barges in at 4 AM or doesn't show up at all."
- "Admin doesn't wait for you to be ready. It piles up like dirty laundry with attitude."

---

### 4. Rhythmic Variety

**Mix short punchy sentences with longer flowing ones. Use single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis.**

**The Pattern:**
1. Short declarative (impact)
2. Longer flowing sentence (context/story)
3. Single-sentence paragraph (emphasis)
4. Return to normal flow

**Example:**
> "She almost quit.
>
> After three years of building her client base, juggling WhatsApp messages at dinner, and somehow still falling behind on invoices, the weight of it all felt unbearable. The passion was still there—buried somewhere beneath the spreadsheets and the stress—but it was getting harder to find.
>
> Then something shifted.
>
> Not overnight. Not dramatically. Just a small change in how she handled bookings. And suddenly, the business that was drowning her became one she could breathe in again."

**Rules:**
- Never more than 3 short sentences in a row (becomes choppy)
- Never more than 2 long sentences in a row (becomes dense)
- Single-sentence paragraphs = maximum 2 per post

---

### 5. Philosophical Closers

**End with a line that lingers. Make it quotable.**

The closing line should feel like wisdom earned, not advice given.

**Weak Closer:**
> "Try these tips and let me know how it goes!"

**Strong Closers:**
> "Sometimes the best business decision is the one that gives you your evenings back."

> "You didn't become a trainer to become an accountant. Remember that."

> "And sometimes, the clients who test you most are the ones who teach you the most about boundaries."

> "Growth isn't always about more clients. Sometimes it's about fewer headaches."

**Patterns:**
- "And sometimes..." (reflective)
- "That's the thing about..." (insight)
- "You didn't [become X] to [do Y]" (values reminder)
- Question that echoes: "When was the last time you trained *yourself*?"

---

## Sentence Starters to Use

These create the intimate, knowing tone:

**Scene-Setting:**
- "There's a moment every trainer knows..."
- "It wasn't the first time, and it wouldn't be the last..."
- "The notification came at the worst possible moment..."

**Empathetic Bridge:**
- "You could feel it building..."
- "And somehow, you're the one apologising..."
- "Not because you failed—because you cared too much."

**Transition & Contrast:**
- "But here's the thing..."
- "And then—quietly, almost accidentally—something changed."
- "It wasn't pretty. But it was honest."

**Reflective:**
- "That's when it hit me..."
- "Looking back now..."
- "The truth nobody tells you about training..."

---

## Words & Phrases to Embrace

**Power Words:**
- quietly, suddenly, somehow, almost
- stubborn, relentless, steady
- gnawed, lingered, crept
- breathe, unravel, shift

**Emotional Anchors:**
- "the kind of [X] that..."
- "not because [expected reason], but because [real reason]"
- "the sort of [X] you only earn by [experience]"

**SA-Specific Flavour:**
- Load shedding references (when relevant)
- "Eish" (sparingly, for emphasis)
- Local gym culture nods
- Rand-based examples (R500, R2000)

---

## Words & Phrases to Avoid

**Generic Social Media Language:**
- "Game-changer" (overused)
- "Here's the thing..." as an opener (save for middle)
- "Let me tell you..." (too salesy)
- "Drop a 🔥 if you agree" (engagement bait)

**Corporate Tone:**
- "Leverage," "optimize," "maximize"
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "Studies show..." (unless specific)

**Filler:**
- "Basically," "Actually," "Honestly"
- "I just wanted to share..."
- "Quick reminder that..."

## Post Structure Template (150-250 words)

```
[ATMOSPHERIC OPENING - 1-2 sentences]
Set the scene. Time, place, feeling. Make it specific.

[THE TENSION - 2-3 sentences]
What's the struggle? Use vivid verbs and em-dash asides.
Make the reader nod in recognition.

[THE SHIFT - 1-2 sentences]
Something changes. A realisation, a tool, a mindset.
Keep it grounded, not miraculous.

[THE INSIGHT - 2-3 sentences]
What does this mean? Connect the specific to the universal.
This is where Refiloe's wisdom shows.

[PHILOSOPHICAL CLOSER - 1 sentence]
Leave them thinking. Make it quotable.

[CTA - 1 sentence, optional]
Soft ask. Question format works best.
```

## Example Transformations

### Before (Generic):
> "Client cancellations are frustrating. Here are 3 ways to reduce them:
> 1. Set clear policies
> 2. Send reminders
> 3. Charge cancellation fees
>
> What strategies work for you? 💪"

### After (Refiloe Style):
> "The message landed at 6:12 AM. 'Can't make it today—sorry!'
>
> No explanation. No reschedule. Just seven words and a sinking feeling in your chest.
>
> You've been here before. The empty slot that was supposed to pay for groceries. The hour you could've given to someone who actually wanted to show up. And somehow—somehow—you're the one feeling guilty for being annoyed.
>
> Here's what changed for me: I stopped treating policies like suggestions. A 24-hour cancellation window isn't mean. It's respect—for your time, your business, and honestly, for your sanity.
>
> The clients who push back? They're telling you something. Listen.
>
> You didn't build this business to be at everyone else's mercy. Remember that."

---

### Before (Generic):
> "Struggling with admin? You're not alone! Many trainers spend hours on paperwork instead of training. Try automating your booking system to save time. ⏰"

### After (Refiloe Style):
> "It was supposed to be a rest day.
>
> Instead, you spent it buried in spreadsheets—client schedules, payment follow-ups, that one booking that somehow duplicated itself three times. The gym stayed empty. The admin pile didn't.
>
> There's a quiet exhaustion that comes from building something you love while drowning in everything around it. The training part? That's why you started. The invoices, the chasing, the endless WhatsApp threads? That's what almost makes you stop.
>
> But here's what I've learned: admin doesn't shrink by ignoring it. It shrinks by automating the parts that don't need you.
>
> Your energy belongs in the sessions, not the spreadsheets.
>
> When was the last time you actually had a rest day?"

## Emoji Guidelines

**Placement:**
- Never in the opening line (breaks atmosphere)
- Maximum 2 per post
- Best at end of CTA or as bullet markers

**Preferred:**
- ✅ for lists/checklists
- 💭 for reflective questions
- 🏋️ for training-specific content
- ❤️ for community/support moments

**Avoid:**
- 🔥 (overused)
- Emoji clusters (💪🔥✨)
- Emojis mid-sentence

## Voice Calibration Check

Before posting, ask:

1. **Does the opening create a scene?** (Not just state a fact)
2. **Would a trainer read this and think "that's exactly how it feels"?**
3. **Is there at least one em-dash aside?**
4. **Does the closer linger?** (Could someone quote it?)
5. **Did I avoid generic social media language?**

If you answered "no" to any of these—revise.
